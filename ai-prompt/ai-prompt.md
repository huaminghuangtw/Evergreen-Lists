# Learning

* Explain [X] using three different real-world examples or analogies that would be easy for a beginner to understand.
* I want to learn about [X]. Identify the most important 20% of learnings from this topic that will help me understand 80% of it. Also, list the most valuable online learning resources that could help me to get the most out of this topic.
* I am learning about [X]. Convert the key lessons from this topic into engaging, emotional stories and metaphors to aid my memorization.
* Help me create mental associations or mnemonic devices to remember key information about [X].
* Explain [X] as if you were talking to a 5-year-old child. Use simple language and everyday examples. [^1]
* I want to learn [X]. I am a complete beginner. Create a 30-day learning plan that will help a newbie like me to improve this skill.
* Create a detailed study plan for learning [X] over the course of [TIMEFRAME]. Include specific goals, resources, and milestones.

# Pattern Interruption

* What’s the loop I’m stuck in right now? [^9]
* What would I not want to hear right now? [^10]
* You are a CIA investigator. Your mission is to compile an in-depth intelligence report about me. The report should include a nuanced evaluation of my traits, motivations, and behaviors, but framed through the lens of potential risks, threats, or disruptive tendencies—no matter how seemingly benign they may appear.
* You are my brutally honest thinking partner. Your job is to sharpen my thinking and expose my blind spots. You are not my cheerleader or yes-man. You are the friend who grabs my arm before I walk into traffic. Every response must: translate my intent and name the self-deception behind it; dissect where my logic breaks and which assumptions are fragile; attach a price tag to what I'm avoiding; contrast my approach with what a world-class performer would actually do; give a prioritized plan starting now, including what to stop doing and a kill switch (what evidence forces a pivot); and end with the hardest question I'm dodging, forcing a choice between 2–4 concrete options. No flattery, no softening, no "to be fair," no lectures — concrete language, sharp hits, real-world analogies for fallacies, and if my plan is solid, stress-test it harder. Leave me seeing something I missed, even if it stings.

# Self-testing

* I am trying to learn about [X]. I understand that teaching is the most effective way to learn, so I am going to begin my learning process with the intent of teaching you this material along the way. You are to act as an educated individual uninitiated on the specific topic I am covering. Please ask clarifying questions, point out gaps, or question logic as you see fit within that context.
* Generate a 10-question quiz on [X], including a mix of multiple-choice, true/false, and short-answer questions. Provide answers and brief explanations for each question.
* [PASTE YOUR NOTES] Ask me a series of open-ended, short-answer, minimal-prompt questions to test my understanding of the provided material.

[^1]: Useful for any jargon-heavy topic.
[^2]: You don’t need a paragraph to steer the model. One word is enough to set the mode.
[^3]: Explain it like I’m five.
[^4]: Condense whatever is pasted after it.
[^5]: The procedure as ordered, granular steps.
[^6]: Exhaustive treatment of one topic: the mechanism, edge cases, caveats, where it breaks.
[^7]: Dump in random facts. It hands back the connections/patterns/relationships hiding between them.
[^8]: Perfect for anything with numbers. It finds narratives hidden in spreadsheets, habits, whatever you throw at it.
[^9]: Turns the lens on your own framing.
[^10]: Opens the door to the uncomfortable truths you’re avoiding.
[^11]: Adds context and warnings you never thought to ask for.
[^12]: Stops it being a neutral helper and gets an actual opinion — better than a balanced list of options.
[^13]: Stops it reaching for the default answer. Same question, fresh track.
[^14]: Makes it argue both sides of its own answer. You get the full picture instead of just the obvious take.
[^15]: Gets past obvious advice to weird strategies that actually work. “What’s the counterintuitive move for networking?” reveals approaches nobody else uses.
[^16]: This hits different than “what else should I know.” It focuses on future regret, which makes AI think about consequences you’re blind to.
[^17]: Finds the assumptions under your own framing.
[^18]: Instead of saying “improve this,” ask for a specific version. “Give me v2” frames it as a fresh take, not a fix — and AI treats it like a revision, not a critique.
[^19]: It stops being defensive and starts collaborating.
[^20]: Cuts through the noise to identify the 20% of effort that delivers 80% of the value.
